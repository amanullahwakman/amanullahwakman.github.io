About Me

I am Amanullah Wakman, a PhD graduate in International Relations from Tarbiat Modares University, Tehran, Iran. My research focuses on foreign policy analysis, neighbourhood policy, regional politics, and political dynamics, with particular emphasis on Afghanistan and its relations with neighbouring and regional powers.

My academic interests include regional security, international political economy, peace and conflict, political stability and instability, soft power, governance, and theoretical approaches in International Relations.

My research examines the logic of change and continuity in foreign and neighbourhood policies and develops multidimensional analytical approaches to understanding political, security, economic, social, ideational, and environmental dynamics.

My recent research focuses particularly on Afghanistan's neighbourhood policy toward Iran and comparative approaches to neighbourhood policy and regional politics.

I have published scholarly articles and presented research at academic conferences on Afghanistan's foreign policy, regional politics, political stability, soft power, international political economy, and International Relations theory.

Doctoral Research

The Logic of Change and Continuity in Afghanistan's Neighbourhood Policy toward Iran: A Comparative Study of the Republic Era and the Second Islamic Emirate Period

This research examines changes and continuities in Afghanistan's neighbourhood policy toward Iran across the Republican period and the Second Islamic Emirate.

The study examines six dimensions of neighbourhood policy: political-diplomatic, security, economic-commercial, cultural-social, identity-ideological, and environmental-natural resources.
